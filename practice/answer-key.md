# Worked checks and decision key

Check the algebra before comparing prose. These are exact checks on teaching fixtures, not evidence about real reservation behavior.

## Chapter checks

1. **Mappings.** At 195 minutes, 3.25 hours cost 38 dollars. A 45-minute request falls outside the contextual domain. Whole-hour bookings cost 20, 28, 36 and 44 dollars; draw four isolated points and restrict the inverse to those prices. $S(J(t))=\sqrt{t-3}$ accepts $t\ge3$; $J(S(t))=\sqrt{t}-3$ accepts $t\ge0$. At 1 only the second is defined, with value −2.
2. **Equations.** $(x-4)(x-5)=0$, so 4 and 5 both give zero. The rational equation simplifies to $x+3=6$ only for $x\ne3$, so it has no solution. For $\sqrt{x+1}=x-1$, require $x\ge1$; squaring gives $x(x-3)=0$. At 3 both sides equal 2; at 0 one gets 1 and −1.
3. **Exponentials.** A 20% decrease has factor $4/5$, so $50(4/5)^2=32$. The weekly growth factor is $\sqrt{3/2}$, and week four is $40(3/2)^2=90$. Week five gives one record in the model; week four gives two. The model stays strictly positive at every finite time, so it never predicts literal clearance.
4. **Systems.** $(14,5)$ uses 43 minutes and fails time; $(14,4)$ consumes 40 and is feasible; $(16,3)$ fails the extended requirement. With 44 minutes, $(16,4)$ serves all 20. With at least six extended and 42 minutes, $2(s+e)+6\le42$ yields at most 18, attained by $(12,6)$. The fractional boundary point $(13.5,5)$ uses exactly 42 minutes and is inside the continuous region, but cannot be booked.
5. **Models.** The first part predicts 16 at 8 dollars, the second predicts 10; only the latter matches the challenge. At 12 dollars the formal second expression gives −6 but has no forecasting authority. A bookable 18 at 6 dollars with four extended uses 40 minutes; eight extended would use 44.

## Worksheet and independent transfer

The endpoint line is $D=28-22(p-2)/7$ on $[2,9]$. It predicts $130/7$ at 5 dollars and $64/7$ at 8 dollars. Its signed held-out residual is $10-64/7=6/7$. The two-slope model is $32-2p$ on $[2,5]$ and $42-4p$ on $(5,9]$. It gives 22 at the join, 10 at 8 dollars and residual 0 there. Fit at the middle observation and the single held-out result both favor the piecewise interpolation in this fixture; neither establishes a general advantage.

At 5 dollars there are 22 requests, exceeding the 19 achievable. With four extended, 18 at 6 dollars implies $(14,4)$ and 40 minutes, so 6 dollars is the minimum feasible modeled whole-dollar price. The rational expression reduces to $x+6$ only at $x\ne6$, and its sole candidate 6 is excluded. The demand model's price restriction comes from scope and observations, not from division by zero.

If at least eight must be extended, $2(s+e)+8\le42$ gives at most 17, attainable at $(9,8)$. At 6 dollars the 18 requests do not fit. At 7 dollars, 14 requests fit as $(6,8)$, using 36 minutes, so 7 dollars is the new minimum. The fitted functions and observed residuals do not change. The decision does because the feasible set changed.
