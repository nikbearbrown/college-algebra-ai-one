# Reservation model worksheet

This case, all observations and all decision constraints are fictional. Start unaided. Use the three fitting rows below without looking at the challenge result in [Chapter 5](../chapters/05-building-and-challenging-models.md) or the answer key. An instructor can give this sheet before Chapter 5. Individuals can cover Part B until their prediction is recorded.

| Price in dollars | Requested reservations |
| ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 28 |
| 5 | 22 |
| 9 | 6 |

## Part A: Write the initial recommendation

1. Construct one line through the two endpoints and a piecewise interpolation through all three points. Show exact slopes and all allowed price intervals. Mark whether each rule gets the middle measurement right.
2. Sketch the two piecewise segments with labeled axes and show a table of both models' predictions at 5 and 8 dollars. Circle your prediction at 8 before using Part B.
3. There are 20 seats and 42 setup minutes. Standard reservations require two minutes, extended require three, and the room must fill at least four extended slots. Assume the studio can allocate the mix accordingly. Find the least whole-dollar price in the piecewise domain that meets demand; prove that the previous price does not.
4. For the equation $(x^2-36)/(x-6)=12$, identify a candidate produced by simplification and explain why it must be excluded. Explain why this is the same kind of contract check as declining to apply the demand rule at 12 dollars, even though the underlying restrictions are different.

## Part B: Release the evidence

An independent count at 8 dollars was **10**. Compute the signed residual (observed minus predicted) for both models. Explicitly decline to forecast outside the observed range. To demonstrate why, calculate what the final segment would give at 12 dollars if extended anyway.

Write a two-sentence conditional recommendation. Then learn that eight extended reservations are required rather than four, with the same total setup time and space. Recompute the maximum and the lowest feasible whole-dollar price in the model. State which part of the decision changed and which part did not.

Keep your initial graph, comparison table, substitution and revised decision in one record. If useful, ask an assistant to challenge one assumption once those are written; a partner can supply the same challenge without AI. Cite the numbers you used to accept or reject the challenge and note any assistance. [Worked key](answer-key.md)
